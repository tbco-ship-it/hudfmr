How the payment standard works

The 90–110% rule

Each public housing authority sets a payment standard for every bedroom size, anywhere from 90% to 110% of the FMR or Small Area FMR (up to 120% with HUD approval as a "success rate" or "exception" standard). This is the maximum subsidy the authority will base its calculation on. Look up your county or ZIP on this site and the 90% and 110% figures are on the page.

What the tenant pays

Housing assistance payment = payment standard (or gross rent, whichever is lower) − total tenant payment. The total tenant payment is the highest of 30% of adjusted monthly income, 10% of gross monthly income, or the authority's minimum rent (usually $0–$50). If the unit's gross rent is above the payment standard, the tenant also pays the difference. At initial lease-up that difference plus the tenant share cannot exceed 40% of adjusted income; after the first year the cap no longer applies.

Rent reasonableness

Independently of the standard, the authority must find the asking rent "reasonable" compared with similar unassisted units nearby. A landlord cannot charge a voucher tenant more than comparable market rent even if the payment standard would cover it.

For landlords

Pricing at or below the payment standard means most voucher households can afford the unit without a gap. Utilities matter: if the tenant pays utilities, the authority subtracts a utility allowance from the standard, so a unit with heat included can list higher than one without.

When standards change

New FMRs take effect October 1. Authorities typically adopt new standards within 90 days; an increase applies to existing tenants at their next annual reexamination, a decrease at the second reexamination after the change.
